About

About me

I am a doctoral student in the Department of Mathematics and Mathematical Statistics at Umeå University, where I am a member of the Mathematical Programming research group. My research focuses on mathematical optimization and machine learning, particularly convex formulations of nonconvex learning problems, conic optimization, and scalable first-order algorithms.

I am currently at EPFL in Lausanne, Switzerland, undertaking a WASP Stint Abroad with the LIONS group from September 2026 to January 2027. During this research visit, I am focusing on non-Euclidean gradient methods for semidefinite programming.

My work connects mathematical structure with computational methods. Topics I have worked on include completely positive and copositive programming for neural network training, adaptive primal-dual methods for constrained convex optimization, and inexact primal-dual algorithms for large-scale semidefinite programming. See my Umeå University staff profile for institutional information and the university publication record.

Inexact PDHG for Large-Scale Semidefinite Programming

Karthik Prakhya and Zimeng Wang

Develops an inexact primal-dual hybrid gradient framework for large-scale semidefinite programming. The work studies adaptive error tolerances for inexact projections and a spectral warm-start strategy for Spectrahedron Frank–Wolfe, with applications to Max-Cut SDPs.

Primal-Dual Adaptive Subgradient Methods

Karthik Prakhya, Zimeng Wang, and Alp Yurtsever

Introduces adaptive primal-dual subgradient methods that use AdaGrad-style step sizes in the dual space and recover primal solutions through averaging of Fenchel-type oracle outputs. The methods adapt to smooth and nonsmooth regimes and are evaluated on optimal transport and Max-Cut semidefinite relaxations.

Exact Convex Reformulations of Linear Neural Networks via Completely Positive Lifting

Karthik Prakhya and Alp Yurtsever

Establishes an exact convex reformulation of deep linear neural network training under squared loss using a generalized completely positive lifting. The lifted formulation represents the nonconvexity induced by linear factorization in a single cone constraint, with dimension independent of network depth and sample count.

Convex Formulations for Training Two-Layer ReLU Neural Networks

Karthik Prakhya, Tolga Birdal, and Alp Yurtsever

Reformulates training sufficiently wide, two-layer ReLU networks as a finite-dimensional convex completely positive program. The paper also develops a semidefinite relaxation and evaluates its tightness and classification performance.
